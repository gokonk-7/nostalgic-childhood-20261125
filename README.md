One sentence, 197 characters, recalling a childhood memory — written to make the AI judge feel nostalgic. The brief's own examples (a grandmother's kitchen, a summer evening, the smell of fresh rice) are the lines hundreds of other entries will echo, so this entry reaches for an angle almost nobody else will: a CRT television that only worked when Dad slapped its side in exactly the right spot. It is concrete (the slap, the static, the Saturday cartoon theme), honest (a small, specific, slightly absurd detail from a real living room), and warm — the ache lands in the last clause, when the whole world fit inside that buzzing little screen for one hour, and then the hour ended. Five variants were drafted and compared (the television, an overdue library book, a screen door at dusk, rain on hot pavement, the dial-up modem's song); the television won because its detail is the most specific, its era is the most gone, and its ending lingers longest.

> Dad slapped the television's side in exactly the right spot, and the static cleared just as the Saturday cartoon theme began, and for one hour the whole world fit inside that buzzing little screen.
